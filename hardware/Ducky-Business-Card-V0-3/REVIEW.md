## Circuit and power

| Circuit | Finding and acceptance check |
|---|---|
| Firmware compatibility | The production-pond image on main still lists PC1/PC2/PC3 as unused. PC1 stays at its reset input state, so firmware supplies no intentional power to the mic or the tag’s I²C side. Build a V0.3-specific image that enables PC1 before listening or I²C, keeps it on through four-blow setup and verified EEPROM completion, and sequences pull-ups, power-off and wake for the actual KC tag. Passive RF reading uses the phone field. |
| Switched rail | R1 and 100 nF give a nominal 47 µs time constant. At 3.2 V the ideal upper bound on initial resistor current is 6.8 mA. Nominal initial slope is 0.068 V/µs. The mic draws about 120 µA typical and the KC tag about 110 µA typical during a 3.3 V EEPROM write under ST’s test conditions. Combined, the estimate is 0.23 mA and a 0.11 V resistor drop. Scope U2 pin 8 against local ground on fresh and weak cells. Verify a continuous rise below 1 V/µs and at least 1.8 V throughout the write, including when a phone is present. |
| LEDs and battery | The 24 anodes connect directly to VCC. Nine CJ3400s switch groups of two or four cathodes. R1 limits peripheral-rail current only. There are no LED series resistors or external gate pull-downs. Startup gates depend on MCU initialization. Current sharing depends on LED forward voltage and source impedance. With the intended CR2032, capture MCU VDD and bank current during reset, the brightest scene and the 12 s hold. The existing 10 MHz image needs at least 2.7 V to stay in its specified speed range. |
| Sleep | A prior physical card drew 25.5 mA during one four-LED hold and 3.0–3.7 mA after great/little luck with the LEDs dark. The meter’s 0.0 mA reading for the other fortunes only establishes a reading below about 1 mA. The proposed PIT re-entry fix compiled in the shared session and lives on a separate branch. Repeat through URL expiry and final sleep on identified firmware, disconnect UPDI, and measure in µA. V0.3 runtime and the <1 µA design target have no bench result. |
| NFC and microphone routing | The KC AC0/AC1 terminals route to J1, PB0/PB1 carry SCL/SDA, and PC0 samples the mic output on ADC1. The bus relies on MCU pull-ups. SCL and SDA have about 64 and 63 mm of routed track. The mic trace is about 66 mm. Check bus rise times and EEPROM readback, and compare the ADC noise floor with the LED banks on and off. U3’s 0.40 mm acoustic opening is the single non-plated hole. Measure NFC tuning/read distance with cell and holder installed. |
| Touch and programming | SW1 uses 7.8 mm sensing copper within an approximately 12 mm keepout. The attached board has a left-entry trace, about 28 mm to PC3, and rear copper is clear within a measured 5.9 mm radius. Qualify front taps, rear grips and nearby RF. The cloud simulation used a test board. UPDI is TP1, ground is TP3, and V0.3 has no dedicated TP2 VCC pad. |
| Battery contact and stencil | BT1 has a 14 mm exposed negative contact. The bottom stencil now omits the negative disc and J1 terminals. Verify that the finished center contact is flat, clean and makes reliable contact with a CR2032. Seven GND via centers lie 8.30–9.06 mm from the cell center. The Gerbers show copper under the rim and no exposed copper in the checked 7.05–10 mm annulus. Inspect mask wear, clearance and cell seating on a sample. |

Electrical limits and wake behavior depend on the purchased device and its errata. ### What to confirm before ordering

1. **U1 and U2 center-pad stencil.** JLC reports 0.17 contact for U1 and U2 and names one small aperture for each. The Gerbers show continuous copper and a full mask opening across each designed center land. There are four separate paste windows on each. Together they cover 64.7% of the 1.70 × 1.70 mm MCU land and 60.4% of the 1.36 × 1.46 mm NFC land. The evidence points to the checker evaluating one window at a time. JLC has not signed off on stencil volume or joint quality. Ask assembly engineering to verify both window patterns, stencil thickness and reflow suitability. [Microchip QFN assembly guidance](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/Appnotes/AN18.15-PCB-Design-Guide-QFN-DQFN-Packages-00001843.pdf) · [ST DFN assembly guidance](https://www.st.com/resource/en/technical_note/tn1171-description-of-ufdfpn5-ufdfpn8-and-wfdfpn8-for-stmicroelectronics-eeproms-and-recommendations-for-use-stmicroelectronics.pdf).
2. **Microphone drill.** U3 has one 0.40 mm NPTH through the acoustic port. Its separate Excellon drill file declares NonPlated and contains a single hit. The viewer labeled both files as plated, including Drill_NPTH_Through.DRL. Ask JLC to verify the port is non-plated and free of mask, paste and solder in its final CAM preview. The screwless holder’s insertion side and contact finish also warrant visual confirmation.
3. **PCB warnings.** The remaining vendor row counts are Sharp trace corner (1), Trace spacing (1), Unlabeled routing check (viewer row 12) (2), Solder mask opening exposing trace (5), Soldermask opening with multiple segments (8), Negative soldermask expansion (4), Via without pad (2), Unconnected via (1). These are feature instances and can share locations. The eight multiple-segment mask findings refer to large circular openings. Four negative-expansion reports involve rotated MCU apertures. Three drill warnings cite a 0.40 mm hole matching the microphone port. Confirm the 0.13–0.15 mm mask-to-trace locations with the actual mask color and inspect the decorative openings on the first board. [JLC published capabilities](https://jlcpcb.com/capabilities/pcb-capabilities).
4. **Physical function.** Check MCU pin 1, LED polarity, and the holder opening in the order preview. On a small first build, measure battery contact reliability, LED peak load, and sleep current, then test microphone response and NFC with the battery installed. Mainline Shared Pond firmware still needs V0.3 switched-rail, GPO and touch support. V0.2 can exercise the separate timed-sleep fix.

## Touch and NFC behavior

On V0.3, the PC3 electrode changes capacitance when touched. Firmware must measure the electrode and handle a touch. Periodic scans, sensitivity and idle current need qualification on a powered card. The NFC tag’s open-drain GPO connects to PC2. With an active pull-up and the proper tag settings, a phone field appearing can signal the MCU while the tag is RF-powered. Field removal needs tag VCC to produce its event. A phone can read a stored URL passively. V0.2 Shared Pond can test timed sleep and phone reading; it has no routed PC3 electrode or PC2 GPO connection. [ST GPO behavior](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/st25dv04kc.pdf).
